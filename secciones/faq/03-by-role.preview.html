Before you ask

Different questions, depending on where you sit.

Energy decisions in a district rarely rest with one person. Here is what each part of the table usually needs to know.

01

Operations and facilities

What it means for the buildings and the team.

Will Rede be making changes to our systems?
No, never directly. All corrections are made by your own controls contractor, with your authorization. We document what needs to change; your team decides what gets implemented.
How much of our time does this take?
Recommissioning is desktop-based and requires no site visits. We need remote access to your building automation system, your drawings, and a controls lead who can implement findings.
What if our buildings are older?
Recommissioning needs modern controls. If you do not have them, the Gap Analysis still works: it runs on utility bills alone and ranks your buildings whatever their age.
02

Finance and business

What it costs and when it pays back.

What does the Gap Analysis cost?
$5,000, four to six weeks from data access to presentation. It analyzes one to two years of utility bills across your portfolio and returns a specific figure for recoverable savings.
What does RUN cost?
1 to 2% of annual energy spend, capped for public sector. Typical payback is two to five months. RUN Insights adds $20,000 a year for two senior engineer sessions.
When do we see savings?
Every recommissioning recommendation is selected for a two-year payback or less, sooner with incentives. Rede identifies and manages the incentive applications.
03

Superintendent and board

What you need to bring to the table.

What are we committing to?
Nothing beyond the diagnostic. The Gap Analysis is a one-time piece of work with no lock-in. Most districts use it to build a case internally, whether or not the next step involves us.
Has this worked for districts like ours?
One Western Canadian school division began with Rede in 2016 and has accumulated $4.4M in savings. A recent Gap Analysis for a large district identified $2.94M in unnecessary annual costs.
Who are we actually working with?
A small team of engineers, working since 2008 in Indigenous, Northern and rural communities across Western Canada. You deal with the people doing the work.
